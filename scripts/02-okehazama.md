### 0:00 – Hook

[SCENE 1: Valley, day. A long line of Imagawa soldiers with spears marching. On screen: Central Japan, 1560]

June 1560. Central Japan. A huge army is marching west. Twenty-five thousand men, maybe more. Banners, horses and spears as far as the eye can see.

[SCENE 2: Young Nobunaga alone in red armor, determined.]

Standing in their way is a young lord with a few thousand soldiers, a bad reputation and one very risky idea.

[SCENE 3: Nobunaga close-up, smug.]

His own advisers think he's finished. His enemies think he's a joke. By the end of the day, one of those two men will be gone, and the history of Japan will change direction.

### 0:36 – The Great Fool of Owari

[SCENE 4: Teenage Nobunaga in a red kimono walking through town, smug. On screen: Oda Nobunaga]

To understand how unlikely this was, you need to meet Oda Nobunaga. Nobunaga was the heir to a small clan in Owari, a province in the middle of Japan. And as a teenager, he was a disaster.

[SCENE 5: Nobunaga with townspeople staring at him.]

He wore his clothes half open. He tied his hair with bright cords. He walked through town eating chestnuts and persimmons, leaning on his friends' shoulders, and spent his days running around with village boys.

[SCENE 6: Two townspeople whispering, Nobunaga small in the background. On screen: "The Great Fool of Owari"]

People had a nickname for him: the Great Fool of Owari.

[SCENE 7: Funeral hall. Nobunaga angry; a shocked retainer.]

At his own father's funeral, the story goes, he showed up dressed like that, walked to the altar, grabbed a handful of incense and threw it at the shrine. Then he walked out.

[SCENE 8: Nobunaga facing two relatives with swords.]

Even his own family didn't trust him. In his first years as clan leader, he had to fight his own relatives just to keep his seat.

[SCENE 9: Nobunaga with a rifle, soldiers with long spears.]

But the fool was paying attention. He trained his men with extra-long spears. He studied the new guns that had recently arrived in Japan with Portuguese traders. And he chose his men for their talent, not their family name.

### 1:55 – The finest archer of the Eastern Sea Road

[SCENE 10: Yoshimoto in an elegant court robe with a fan, smug. On screen: Imagawa Yoshimoto]

Now meet his neighbor, Imagawa Yoshimoto. Yoshimoto was everything Nobunaga was not. He was rich, respected and refined.

[SCENE 11: Yoshimoto with two bowing retainers. On screen: "Finest archer of the Eastern Sea Road"]

He ruled three provinces along the Pacific coast and loved the elegant culture of the imperial court in Kyoto. People called him the finest archer of the Eastern Sea Road, a title that meant he was the strongest lord in the region.

[SCENE 12: Yoshimoto now in armor, pointing his fan forward.]

And in the summer of 1560, Yoshimoto decided to march west.

[SCENE 13: Narrator shrugging on a plain background. On screen: Why? Historians still argue.]

Historians still debate why. Maybe he wanted to reach Kyoto and take control of the government. Maybe he just wanted Owari. Either way, Nobunaga's land was right in his path.

[SCENE 14: Left: many small Imagawa soldiers. Right: a few Oda soldiers. On screen: 25,000 vs 2,000]

Yoshimoto's army is usually counted at about twenty-five thousand men. Some sources say more. Nobunaga could gather maybe two or three thousand. On paper, this wasn't a war. It was a formality.

### 2:53 – The forts fall

[SCENE 15: Burning fort on the horizon, Imagawa soldiers advancing.]

Along the border, Nobunaga had built a line of small forts. Yoshimoto's army hit them one by one.

[SCENE 16: Young commander Motoyasu in blue armor, determined. On screen: Matsudaira Motoyasu, age 17]

One of the commanders leading that attack was a seventeen-year-old named Matsudaira Motoyasu. He had grown up as a hostage in the Imagawa household, and now he fought for Yoshimoto.

[SCENE 17: Motoyasu smug; narrator points at him.]

Remember that name. He'll come back later, under a different one.

[SCENE 18: Messenger running through Kiyosu at dawn.]

Before dawn on the nineteenth day of the fifth month, the border forts of Marune and Washizu came under attack. Smoke was rising over the border, and messengers were riding hard to Nobunaga's castle at Kiyosu.

### 3:35 – Fifty years of human life

[SCENE 19: War council the night before. Worried generals, relaxed Nobunaga.]

The night before, Nobunaga's generals had gathered for a war council. They wanted a plan. Should they hide in the castle? Should they ask for peace?

[SCENE 20: Nobunaga waving the generals off; generals shocked.]

Nobunaga just chatted about everyday things. Then he told everyone to go home and get some sleep. His generals walked out convinced their lord had lost his mind.

[SCENE 21: Messenger bursts in; Nobunaga wakes, surprised.]

Then, before dawn, the news arrived. The border forts were under attack.

[SCENE 22: Nobunaga dancing with a fan, eyes closed. On screen: Atsumori]

Nobunaga got up and did something strange. He performed a dance from a famous Noh play called Atsumori, and sang its most famous lines.

[SCENE 23: Close-up of Nobunaga singing, eyes closed. On screen: "Fifty years of human life..."]

Fifty years of human life, compared to the world below heaven, are like a dream, an illusion. In other words: life is short. Don't waste it.

[SCENE 24: Nobunaga in armor rushing out, a few riders behind.]

Then he called for his armor, ate his breakfast standing up and rode out of the castle. At first, only a handful of riders could keep up with him.

[SCENE 25: Nobunaga bowing at the torii gate. On screen: Atsuta Shrine]

He stopped at Atsuta Shrine to pray. Soldiers kept arriving until he had perhaps two thousand men. Some accounts say a little more.

[SCENE 26: Nobunaga and soldiers watching smoke from the burning forts.]

From the hills, he could see the smoke of his burning forts.

### 4:57 – The storm

[SCENE 27: Yoshimoto in armor resting in the valley with his fan, smug. On screen: Okehazama]

Meanwhile, Yoshimoto was in a great mood. His army had won the morning. So around midday, he stopped to rest in a narrow valley called Okehazama.

[SCENE 28: Imagawa soldiers relaxing, helmets off.]

Some of his troops spread out to guard the area. Others celebrated. Yoshimoto sat down for a meal. He was confident. Nobody attacks an army ten times their size.

[SCENE 29: Sudden storm over the valley. Imagawa soldiers surprised.]

And then the weather turned. A violent storm swept over the hills. Rain came down so hard that soldiers couldn't see more than a few steps ahead. One chronicle says the wind was strong enough to knock down trees.

[SCENE 30: Oda soldiers moving through the rain with spears.]

Yoshimoto's men took cover. Nobunaga's men kept moving.

[SCENE 31: Narrator, thinking. On screen: Around the side, or straight ahead?]

Historians still argue about exactly what happened next. The famous version says Nobunaga circled through the hills and came down on the camp from behind. Some scholars now think he simply marched straight at the enemy, hidden by the storm. Either way, the result was the same.

[SCENE 32: Rain stops. Nobunaga charges with a sword.]

The moment the rain stopped, Nobunaga's men charged.

[SCENE 33: Imagawa camp in chaos, soldiers running.]

The Imagawa camp fell into chaos. Soldiers were scattered, armor was off, and many had no idea where the enemy was coming from.

[SCENE 34: Yoshimoto stepping out, annoyed, with his fan.]

According to one account, when Yoshimoto heard the noise, he thought his own men had started a fight and stepped out to tell them to be quiet. It wasn't his own men.

[SCENE 35: Oda samurai facing Yoshimoto, who is shocked.]

His bodyguards fought hard, but the attackers broke through. Two of Nobunaga's samurai reached Yoshimoto himself. The strongest lord in the region fell in the mud of a small valley.

[SCENE 36: Imagawa soldiers retreating in every direction.]

With their leader gone, the huge Imagawa army simply fell apart. Units retreated in every direction. The invasion was over in an afternoon.

### 6:54 – Why it worked

[SCENE 37: Diagram-like: few Oda soldiers vs a small Imagawa group. On screen: 1. Only fight part of the army]

So how did two thousand beat twenty-five thousand? First, Nobunaga never had to fight all twenty-five thousand. Yoshimoto's army was spread out over miles of roads and forts. At Okehazama, Nobunaga only had to beat the part protecting the lord.

[SCENE 38: Nobunaga walking fast. On screen: 2. Speed]

Second, speed. While everyone else was planning, Nobunaga was moving.

[SCENE 39: Rain over the valley, Nobunaga smiling. On screen: 3. Luck]

And third, luck. That storm arrived at exactly the right moment. But luck only helps people who are already in position to use it.

### 7:27 – What came next

[SCENE 40: Townspeople cheering Nobunaga.]

The battle made the Great Fool of Owari famous overnight.

[SCENE 41: Nobunaga and Motoyasu facing each other, both bowing slightly: an alliance. On screen: Tokugawa Ieyasu]

And remember that seventeen-year-old commander, Matsudaira Motoyasu? With the Imagawa clan in chaos, he broke free, went home and two years later became Nobunaga's ally. He later changed his name to Tokugawa Ieyasu.

[SCENE 42: Older Ieyasu in an official robe with a scroll. On screen: Tokugawa shogunate, 1603]

Yes, that Tokugawa. About forty years after Okehazama, Ieyasu would win the struggle for Japan and found the Tokugawa shogunate, the government that gave Japan the long peace we saw in our last episode. The same peace that left the forty-seven ronin with no wars to fight.

[SCENE 43: Nobunaga with a scroll, determined. On screen: 天下布武 Tenka fubu]

As for Nobunaga, he kept going. He took new provinces, marched into Kyoto and began using a personal seal with four words on it: Tenka fubu, roughly "the whole realm under military rule." The fool now wanted all of Japan.

[SCENE 44: Nobunaga surprised at night, a shadowy general behind him. On screen: Honnō-ji, 1582]

He came very close. Within about twenty years, Nobunaga controlled much of central Japan. Then, in 1582, at a temple in Kyoto called Honnō-ji, one of his own generals turned against him. That's a story for next time.
