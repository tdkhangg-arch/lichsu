FATHER: Quick summary. He became king at twenty. By thirty, he ruled most of the known world. And he never lost a major battle.

SON: Never? Maybe his enemies were just weak.

[SCENE 4: Father leans in.]

FATHER: The opposite. One city was so sure it was untouchable that it laughed at him. It stood on an island, out at sea. No navy, no chance. Guess what Alexander did.

[SCENE 5: Son, guessing.]

SON: Borrowed some boats?

[SCENE 6: Father, with a straight face. Son's jaw drops.]

FATHER: Nope. He told his army to fill in the sea. Turn the island into land, and walk right up to the walls.

### 0:52 – The island city

[SCENE 7: Map: Alexander's route from Macedonia past Issus, down the coast to Tyre. On screen: 332 BC]

332 BC. Alexander has just beaten the Persian king at Issus, and he's marching down the coast of the Mediterranean. City after city opens its gates to him. All except one: Tyre.

[SCENE 8: The island city of Tyre rising out of the sea, its walls lined with defenders; the mainland beach on the left. On screen: Tyre]

Tyre was one of the richest ports in the world, home of the Phoenician sea traders and of a purple dye so expensive that only kings could wear it.

[SCENE 9: The walls rise straight out of the water; a caption '150 ft?' next to them. On screen: Half a mile out at sea]

And it was built on an island, about half a mile offshore. Its walls rose straight out of the sea. Ancient writers claimed they stood a hundred and fifty feet high on the side facing the land.

[SCENE 10: On top of the wall: smug Tyrian soldiers eating grapes and laughing at the tiny Macedonian army on the far beach.]

Without a strong navy, attacking Tyre looked impossible, and the Tyrians knew it. When Alexander asked to enter the city to make a sacrifice at its temple, they told him no.

[SCENE 11: Tyrian soldiers on the wall turn their backs to the mainland, arms crossed. Nothing graphic.]

Then, when he sent heralds to talk peace, the Tyrians executed them and threw them from the walls, in full view of his army.

### 2:00 – A road across the sea

[SCENE 12: Alexander on the beach, red cape blowing, staring at the island. Officers behind him look worried.]

Alexander didn't scream or storm off. He called in his best engineers and gave an order that left his officers speechless.

[SCENE 13: Alexander points at the island; a dashed gold arrow shows the planned road across the water. An engineer holds a plan. On screen: “Build a road to the island.”]

His plan, in plain words: if we can't sail to the island, we'll build a road to it. A causeway of stone and timber, straight across the sea.

[SCENE 14: Father and son step onto the beach and look out at Tyre (son asks).]

SON: Wait. A road half a mile long, across open sea? In 332 BC? They didn't even have concrete!

[SCENE 15: Father and son on the beach (father explains).]

FATHER: Exactly. That's why the Tyrians watched from their walls like it was a comedy show. They just didn't realize who they were dealing with. Alexander didn't do detours.

### 2:42 – The engineers' war

[SCENE 16: Soldiers carry stones down the beach and dump them into the sea; the causeway starts to grow. On screen: Building the causeway]

So the work began. Alexander tore down the old town of Tyre on the mainland and dumped its stones into the sea. His men cut cedar trees in the mountains of Lebanon and drove them into the seabed.

[SCENE 17: Weeks later: the causeway is halfway to the island, workers busy along it. On screen: About 200 ft wide]

Day after day, the causeway crept away from the beach. It was about two hundred feet wide, wide enough to march an army down.

[SCENE 18: From the wall: the causeway is coming closer. The Tyrians have stopped laughing; one has dropped his grapes.]

Up on the walls, the Tyrians stopped laughing. And as the causeway reached deeper water, their archers began shooting down at the workers.

[SCENE 19: Two tall wooden siege towers, covered in hides, stand at the end of the causeway. On screen: Siege towers]

Alexander's answer: two huge wooden siege towers at the end of the causeway, taller than the walls, covered with raw hides so they wouldn't catch fire. Now his archers could shoot down at the Tyrians.

### 3:35 – Fire and storm

[SCENE 20: A Tyrian ship packed with brushwood and pitch, set ablaze, drifts toward the towers. On screen: The fire ship]

The Tyrians struck back. They took a big cargo ship, packed it with dry branches, pitch and sulfur, and towed it toward the causeway. Then they set it on fire and let the wind carry it in.

[SCENE 21: Both siege towers burn; smoke over the sea. No one shown hurt.]

The fire ship slammed into the end of the causeway, and both towers went up in flames. Then Tyrian boats rowed out and tore down whatever was left standing.

[SCENE 22: A storm lashes the sea; the end of the causeway has been washed away.]

And as if that wasn't enough, a storm swept in and washed away a big part of months of work.

### 4:13 – Start again, bigger

[SCENE 23: Alexander alone on the beach at sunset, staring at the ruined causeway, jaw set.]

Most generals would have packed up and gone home. To Alexander, it only meant one thing: the causeway hadn't been wide enough.

[SCENE 24: A new, wider causeway; many more workers. On screen: Wider. Stronger.]

He started over, building a new and much wider causeway. And he fixed his biggest weakness. He went and got himself a navy.

[SCENE 25: Warships with red and white sails surround the island; the Tyrians on the walls look shocked. On screen: About 200 warships]

Cities he had already taken, like Sidon and Byblos, sent their warships. The kings of Cyprus brought over a hundred more. Now Tyre was the one surrounded, by sea.

[SCENE 26: Father and son on the beach, ships behind them (son asks).]

SON: So he built a whole navy just to finish his road?

[SCENE 27: Father and son on the beach (father gives his take).]

FATHER: Pretty much. Here's what made Alexander so dangerous: he treated a failure as information, not as the end. The fire ship taught him he needed control of the water. So he went and got it.

[SCENE 28: Tyrians pour glowing sand from the walls; a Macedonian soldier hops about, shaking it out of his armor (cartoon gag, nothing graphic). On screen: Red-hot sand]

The Tyrians grew desperate, and creative. They heated sand in bronze shields until it glowed, then poured it down on the attackers. It slipped under armor, and men tore off their gear just to get it out.

[SCENE 29: Ships anchored by the walls; Tyrian divers in the water cut the anchor ropes.]

Tyrian divers swam out and cut the anchor ropes of Alexander's ships, so he switched to iron chains. Every trick only slowed him down.

### 5:33 – The fall of Tyre

[SCENE 30: After seven months the causeway touches the wall; ships with battering rams line up below the southern wall. On screen: July 332 BC]

After about seven months, the causeway finally reached the walls of Tyre. But the final blow came from the sea. Ships carrying battering rams broke open a section of the southern wall.

[SCENE 31: Alexander, helmet on, climbs onto the wall at the head of his men; Tyrian defenders fall back.]

Alexander himself led the assault through the breach. After seven months of resistance, the city fell in a single day.

[SCENE 32: A quiet parchment card with the ancient figures. Serious tone, no images of violence.]

The anger built up over seven months was terrible. According to the ancient historian Arrian, about eight thousand Tyrians fell in the fighting, and some thirty thousand people were sold into slavery.

### 6:11 – A new map

[SCENE 33: Top-down map: the island of Tyre, the mainland, and Alexander's causeway between them. On screen: Tyre, 332 BC]

And here's the craziest part. Alexander's causeway never went away.

[SCENE 34: The same map today: sand has piled up on both sides of the causeway, joining the island to the coast; a modern town covers it. On screen: Tyre today]

Over the centuries, sand carried along the coast piled up against it, wider and wider. Today, Tyre isn't an island anymore. It's joined to the coast of Lebanon by a strip of land, and geologists who drilled into it found Alexander's road still buried inside.

[SCENE 35: Alexander on the finished causeway, arms crossed, very pleased with himself.]

All because Alexander didn't like taking detours. He changed the map of the world.
