## Description
332 BC. Every city on the coast opened its gates to Alexander the Great, except one. Tyre sat on an island half a mile out at sea, behind walls the ancient writers said were 150 feet tall. The Tyrians laughed at him. So Alexander decided to build a road across the sea.

A father tells his son the story of the Siege of Tyre: why Alexander couldn't leave Tyre behind, the causeway of cedar and stone, the siege towers, the fire ship and the storm, the cranes that pulled boulders out of the sea, and why the island of Tyre is still joined to the mainland today.

Sources: Arrian, Anabasis 2.15-2.24; Plutarch, Life of Alexander 24-25; Marriner et al., "Holocene morphogenesis of Alexander the Great's isthmus at Tyre in Lebanon", PNAS (2007).
